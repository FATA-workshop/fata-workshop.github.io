1 / About

Why FATA?

The First Ask Then Act (FATA) Workshop promotes an interdisciplinary dialogue across social sciences, psychology, philosophy of language, computer science, and Natural Language Processing with human-centered approaches, bringing together researchers, practitioners and stakeholders throughout the research process.

As foundation models increasingly mediate communication and access to information, it becomes essential to question whose knowledge and perspectives are represented, whose needs are prioritized, and who remains excluded or exposed to harm.

Against this backdrop, the workshop highlights community-based and participatory methods that recognize underrepresented communities and low-resources language speakers as essential knowledge holders, necessary to developing more equitable, inclusive, and socially responsible language technologies.

2 / Submissions

Call for Papers

By providing access through textual and speech modalities, foundation models increasingly shape social interactions, access to information, and decision-making, yet they can reproduce and reinforce existing inequalities through biased data, design, and deployment practices. Despite growing attention to bias and fairness in Natural Language Processing, research often overlooks the structural conditions that affect marginalized communities, including their silencing, underrepresentation, and exclusion, and leading to unintended forms of discrimination.

The growing adoption of Large Language Models (LLMs) in language technologies and content moderation further amplifies these concerns, raising fundamental questions about the social implications of NLP systems: Whose knowledge is represented? Whose needs and perspectives are prioritized? Which communities remain underserved, misrepresented, or exposed to harm?

Addressing these challenges requires moving beyond purely technical approaches towards socially grounded, context-aware NLP that incorporates diverse perspectives and situated knowledge and needs of affected communities. This workshop aims to foster interdisciplinary dialogue across social sciences, psychology, philosophy of language, computer science, and NLP, bringing together researchers and practitioners to critically assess existing approaches, and explore alternative frameworks and practices for developing more equitable, inclusive, and socially responsible language technologies.

Topics of Interest

We welcome contributions on (but not limited to) the following topics:

  • Community-based approaches: Challenges, limitations, and opportunities of participatory design and community engagement, and new frameworks and methodologies across fields.
  • Data collection and annotation practices: Collaborative data collection, crowdsourcing platforms, community-driven annotation, limitations of current annotation practices, and the role of linguistic technologies in mitigating bias and addressing under-representation.
  • Intersectionality as a framework: Integrating intersecting identities and perspectives into computational linguistics, with attention to bias, exclusion, marginalization, and representation.
  • Multiple-perspective annotation schemas and tools: Schemas, guidelines, and tools that capture disagreement, subjectivity, and diverse viewpoints while addressing quality, consistency, and representativeness.
  • Bias, fairness, and representation: Methods for identifying, understanding, and mitigating bias in language data and NLP systems, especially where it affects marginalized communities.

We particularly encourage interdisciplinary, community-based and participatory work that involves underrepresented communities and speakers of low-resource languages across the whole research pipeline.

Submission Types

The workshop will accept the following types of submissions. Page limits exclude references and appendices. Accepted papers will be given an additional page of content to address reviewer comments.

Type Length Proceedings Notes
Long paper Up to 8 pages Archival Previously published papers cannot be accepted.
Short paper Up to 4 pages Archival Previously published papers cannot be accepted.
Position paper, demo, annotation scheme, checklist Up to 8 pages —
Non-archival submission Up to 2 pages Non-archival To summarise and showcase in-progress work and work published elsewhere.

Submission & Review

How it works

Submission is electronic, using OpenReview. Papers must be in English (PDF). All papers undergo double-blind review by at least two reviewers, with final acceptance decisions made by the workshop organisers based on the scores provided.
